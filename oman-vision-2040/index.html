01Event context

Event context

The event

COMEX Oman 2025

One of Oman's leading annual technology exhibitions, COMEX brings together government entities, innovators and businesses to showcase advancements in technology and digital transformation.

The vision

Oman Vision 2040

A national development framework outlining Oman's long-term ambitions across economic diversification, technology, sustainability and social development.

The client

MTCIT

The Ministry of Transport, Communications and Information Technology plays a key role in advancing Oman's digital transformation and technological infrastructure.

For COMEX 2025, we collaborated with MTCIT to bring Oman's Vision 2040 initiatives to life through an interactive holographic installation.

02The challenge

How do we communicate a national vision in a matter of seconds?

The challenge was to make complex initiatives easy to discover, understand and explore through short interactions.

  1. ALimited visitor attention

    Unlike a website, an exhibition doesn't guarantee a visitor's attention. People are constantly moving, exploring different installations and deciding what interests them.

  2. BExtensive information

    We had an extensive amount of information to communicate, but very little time to engage each visitor.

  3. CTransparent holographic display

    An unfamiliar medium — a transparent, touch-interactive holographic display.

  4. MTWTFSS

    DOne-week timeline

    A timeline of just one week.

03My role

My role went beyond designing the interface.

I researched individual initiatives, structured the information, explored different content formats and designed the end-to-end interactive experience.

I also conceptualised and produced AI-generated imagery, films and podcasts, taking ownership of both the interface and the content visitors would interact with.

04Research & approach

Understanding the content before designing the experience

Before starting with the interface, I spent time understanding the Vision 2040 framework and the initiatives associated with it.

For each initiative, I focused on three questions:

  1. Q1

    What is Oman working towards?

  2. Q2

    What progress has been made?

  3. Q3

    How can we communicate this to someone unfamiliar with the subject?

This helped me identify the most relevant information and explore how it could be communicated visually.

Breaking down a national vision

We structured the experience around five pillars, with individual parameters representing different areas of development.

  1. Vision 2040
  2. Five Pillars
  3. Parameters
  4. Individual Stories

Rather than taking visitors through a fixed narrative, we allowed them to explore topics independently, based on their interests and the time they had available.

05Design explorations

Finding the right way to tell the story

The information architecture gave us a starting point, but how we presented that information evolved through three iterations.

  1. 01Information-led
  2. 02Multimedia
  3. 03Motion-led

Iteration 01

Information-led interface

We initially approached the experience much like a digital information platform, using content cards, descriptions and supporting imagery.

While the structure made the information easy to navigate, visitors still had to spend time reading to understand each initiative.

The information was organised, but the format wasn't quite right for an exhibition.

Iteration 02

Multimedia experience

We explored a richer content experience through statistics, data visualisations, success stories and AI-generated podcasts.

Hardware constraint

The display was transparent.

This iteration also introduced an important hardware constraint. Since the display was transparent and the surroundings were visible through it, the interface required white to maintain visibility and legibility.

Illustration Simulated surroundings — not footage of the installation. Uses an original Iteration 02 screen and a final screen.

Although multimedia offered more ways to explore the content, visitors still needed time to read, listen and interpret the information.

Iteration 03

Motion-led experience

Instead of adding more ways to consume information, we reconsidered how much information visitors needed in the first place.

Retained

PillarParameterStory

We retained the existing information architecture…

…but replaced detailed explanations with short visual films.

This allowed us to communicate individual initiatives in seconds while making better use of the holographic medium.

06The final experience

An experience built around exploration

The final interface allowed visitors to navigate between five pillars, select individual parameters and discover the corresponding initiatives through short films.

The five pillars

    Minimal, by intent

    We kept the interface minimal, with clear navigation, larger touch targets and a visual hierarchy suited to the transparent display.

      07Visual storytelling

      Turning complex initiatives into visual narratives

      How do you visually communicate something like cybersecurity or digital transformation in just a few seconds?

      For each parameter, I researched the initiative, identified its central message and developed a visual concept around it.

      I drew inspiration from Oman's architecture, landscapes, people and infrastructure to make the films feel relevant to the country rather than relying on generic representations of technology.

        Why 7–8seconds

        The duration of the films was a deliberate design decision.

        Each film focused on a single idea, allowing visitors to understand the essence of an initiative without committing to a lengthy video.

        It also made exploring multiple topics possible within a short visit to the installation.

          Film gallery

            08AI-assisted creative production

            From research to the final film

            With multiple initiatives to cover and just one week for the entire project, traditional media production would have been difficult.

            I used generative AI to explore visual concepts, experiment with compositions and create motion sequences.

            The generated clips were then edited, combined and enhanced with sound to create the final films.

              Each stage involved experimentation and refinement to maintain visual consistency and ensure the films communicated their intended message.

              Tools used

              AI helped accelerate production, while the storytelling, art direction, editing and creative decisions remained central to my role.

              09Outcome

              Outcome

              In one week, we developed an interactive holographic experience for COMEX 2025 that combined structured information, touch interactions and AI-generated films.

              Rather than presenting Vision 2040 through lengthy descriptions, the final experience allowed visitors to discover Oman's initiatives through short, independent visual stories.

              10Reflection

              Reflection

              What made this project particularly interesting was that the design extended beyond the screen.

              The physical surroundings, the transparency of the display and the way visitors moved through the exhibition influenced both the interface and the content.

              Looking back at the three iterations, the biggest shift wasn't in how we organised information, but in how we chose to communicate it.

              Sometimes, designing a better experience means showing less.
